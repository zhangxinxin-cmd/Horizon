## [OpenAI Claims AI Agents Solved Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem, Precipitating Priority Dispute](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced that an unreleased internal frontier model, running in a swarm of around 10,000 AI agents, had resolved the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem and formalized the result in the Lean proof assistant. The proposed solution is a counter-example showing a breakdown of smooth solutions, and OpenAI said it would decline the $1 million Millennium Prize if the result were officially recognized. If verified, this would be the first time AI has resolved one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems and could fundamentally transform how mathematical research is conducted. It also raises serious ethical and priority concerns, because OpenAI launched its effort only after hearing rumors about earlier, related work by Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge and NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster. OpenAI reports that the agents sent approximately 2.7 million messages and consumed about 130 billion output tokens on the Navier-Stokes problem alone; across all attempted problems the totals were 4.9 million messages and 300 billion tokens. The result is a proposed counter-example to the smoothness assertion, not a proof that smooth global solutions always exist, and external mathematicians along with the Clay Mathematics Institute have not yet verified it.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 8, 23:55

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations describe the motion of viscous fluids and underpin fields from meteorology to aerospace engineering, but whether smooth solutions always exist in three dimensions was an open question. In 2000, the Clay Mathematics Institute designated the problem as one of seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million prize, and as of 2026 only the Poincaré conjecture had been officially solved. The OpenAI announcement is a proposed counter-example rather than a smoothness proof, and it has yet to be checked by outside mathematicians. The work uses Lean, an interactive proof assistant that mechanically verifies mathematical arguments.

## [GPT-6 Astra, Looped Transformers, and Hidden Reasoning](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Sebastian Raschka's magazine article analyzes OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra in the context of looped (recurrent-depth) transformers and hidden reasoning. It examines how these architectural ideas could change chain-of-thought reasoning and user-facing model performance. This matters because looped transformers and hidden or latent reasoning could significantly reduce inference cost and latency by replacing long chain-of-thought tokens with internal computation. It also shapes the competitive conversation around GPT-6 Astra versus alternatives such as Sol, especially for computer-use and agentic tasks. The community discussion references Will Merrill's arXiv work (arXiv:2310.07923) on how much chain-of-thought is needed for different computational problems, and notes that universal transformers are an earlier version of looping weight-tied transformers. Some users report that GPT-6 Astra felt degraded after an update, while others praise real-time computer-use demos such as using MSPAINT.

hackernews · ModelForge · Sep 9, 14:37 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49627370)

**Background**: A looped transformer, also called a recurrent-depth or recursive transformer, reuses the same transformer block for multiple iterations instead of stacking entirely new layers, trading training-time depth for flexible inference-time computation. Hidden or latent reasoning (as in Coconut-style continuous thought) means the model performs intermediate computations directly in its hidden state rather than emitting visible chain-of-thought text. GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI's recently introduced model, positioned for end-to-end computer use, browser automation, and agentic work. These concepts matter because they could make models both cheaper to run and harder for humans to audit.

## [Qwen 3.8 Reasoning Prefills Echo GPT-5.5 Pro, Raising Distillation Questions](https://gist.github.com/wsxiaoys/e0286dc6bb624ff5fdf49e7f4c528ba3) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new analysis posted on Hacker News shows that Qwen 3.8's publicly visible reasoning traces closely follow the captured 'reasoning prefills' of GPT-5.5 Pro, and comments debate whether this comes from distillation or shared training data. The analysis draws on a technique from the 'stolen-thoughts' paper that first recovers chain-of-thought text from proprietary models. The finding could cast new light on how Chinese open-weight models such as Qwen reach their strong capabilities, and whether they are trained on outputs from proprietary systems without permission. That has major implications for AI research, open-source licensing, and the competitive dynamics between major labs. The exploit in question captures readable chain-of-thought from OpenAI and Anthropic models, and the analysis runs a benchmark with a state-of-the-art model, recovers its CoT, then feeds the first 1% of that trace to an open model as the start of its own CoT. One commenter notes that Qwen 3.8 0902 was trained after the 'stolen thoughts' paper was released on August 10, so it could simply have memorized those leaked traces.

hackernews · wsxiaoys · Sep 9, 17:24 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49630026)

**Background**: LLM inference is split into two phases: a prefill phase where the model processes the prompt in parallel, and a decode phase where it generates each output token one at a time. Here the term 'reasoning prefills' means the beginning of a recovered hidden chain-of-thought that is fed into another model as a prompt prefix. Knowledge distillation is a common method of transferring behavior from a large teacher model to a smaller student model, which is why researchers watch closely for signs of distillation.

It also points to a broader trend of bringing compute-intensive desktop tools to the browser. Software-defined radio (SDR) implements radio functions like modulation and filtering in software rather than dedicated analog hardware. WebAssembly is a portable binary format that allows high-performance code written in languages like C++ to run inside web browsers. Running GNU Radio in the browser combines these technologies, making SDR exploration accessible without complex local setup.

## [Terence Tao Warns AI Could Undermine Open Mathematical Sharing](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/9/terence-tao/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In a Mathstodon post, Terence Tao warned that the pool of good, fruitful open problems is now being 'mined in a non-renewable fashion.' He said that even the rumor of someone working on a problem can trigger a massive AI-powered effort to solve it before the original researcher can finish, which may push people to stop sharing research directions. As one of the world's most prominent mathematicians, Tao's warning adds weight to an emerging worry that AI will not only solve problems but also change how researchers behave. If sharing promising directions becomes strategically costly, centuries of open-science norms could erode, slowing progress in mathematics and AI research alike. Tao frames good open problems as a finite resource whose value can be destroyed by premature, AI-driven competition. The concern is not that AI has solved everything, but that the mere prospect of such rapid flattening may make secrecy rational, reversing generations of collaborative practice.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 9, 00:20
