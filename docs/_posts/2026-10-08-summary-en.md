## [Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2026 Goes to Kagan and Soai for Asymmetric Catalysis](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2026/press-release/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded to Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai for their work on asymmetric catalysis and autocatalysis, honoring decades of research into how chemical reactions can selectively produce one mirror-image form of a molecule and, in Soai's case, even amplify that preference on their own. Asymmetric catalysis is central to producing single-enantiomer pharmaceuticals and fine chemicals, where the two mirror-image forms of a molecule can have completely different biological effects, and autocatalysis offers a plausible chemical route to the origin of biological homochirality — life's near-universal preference for one-handed molecules. Kagan is widely known for developing C2-symmetric chiral ligands and practical asymmetric methods, while Soai is celebrated for the Soai reaction, an asymmetric autocatalytic reaction in which a product amplifies its own chirality, allowing minuscule chiral imbalances to grow into nearly enantiopure products; notably, that feat of self-amplifying chirality had previously been achieved only by living systems.

hackernews · sasvari · Oct 7, 09:51 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49990470)

**Background**: Chirality describes molecules that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other, much like a left and right hand; both forms, called enantiomers, have identical physical properties in most respects but can behave very differently in biological settings. Asymmetric catalysis uses a chiral catalyst to preferentially accelerate the formation of one enantiomer over the other, which is essential for making drugs that are safe and effective. Autocatalysis is a reaction in which a product also acts as a catalyst for its own formation, a mechanism that can sharply amplify small initial imbalances. Together these ideas bear on the puzzle of why life uses almost exclusively left-handed amino acids and right-handed sugars.

## [Margaret Hamilton, Apollo Software Lead, Dies at 89](https://news.mit.edu/2026/margaret-hamilton-computing-pioneer-dies-1007) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Margaret Hamilton, the computing pioneer who led the development of the Apollo Guidance Computer's flight software and popularized the term 'software engineering', has died at the age of 89, according to MIT News. She was the director of the Software Engineering Division at MIT's Instrumentation Laboratory (now Draper Laboratory), where her team's code guided the Apollo missions, including the critical moments of the Apollo 11 lunar landing. Hamilton is a foundational figure in the history of computing: her work demonstrated that software could be engineered to be reliable enough for life-critical missions, and her coinage of 'software engineering' helped establish the discipline as a legitimate engineering field rather than an afterthought. Her death marks the loss of one of the last directly connected voices to the Apollo-era software effort that shaped modern aerospace and software practices. Hamilton developed the Apollo onboard flight software that employed priority-based scheduling and error detection and recovery routines, most famously allowing the Apollo 11 landing to continue when the computer was overloaded with spurious radar data. She reportedly brought her young daughter Lauren to work, whose experimentation in the simulator led Hamilton to add code that would prevent a mission abort if the astronaut selected the wrong program during flight.

hackernews · muglug · Oct 7, 21:16 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49998895)

**Background**: In the early 1960s, software was widely regarded as a secondary, almost clerical activity compared with hardware design, and the discipline lacked formal engineering rigor. The Apollo Guidance Computer was an extremely constrained machine by modern standards, with only a few kilobytes of memory, so its flight software had to be written with extreme care and efficiency. Hamilton's team at the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory wrote the code by hand and wove it into core rope memory, literally threading wires through magnetic cores — a process that inspired the famous photograph of her standing beside a stack of printed listings. The term 'software engineering' was meant to signal that building software deserved the same seriousness and standards as building physical hardware.

A commenter also noted she coined the term 'software engineer' and speculated, drawing on Steven Levy's 'Hackers', that she may have been the programmer behind a late-night TX-0 incident that disrupted an academic weather simulation for Prof. Edward Lorenz.

## [Chrome Ships JPEG XL Support, Reversing Earlier Deprecation](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/jpeg-xl-in-chrome) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google is shipping JPEG XL (JXL) support in Chrome, reversing its earlier decision to deprecate and remove the format from Chromium. The move coincides with Firefox's imminent stable rollout, which takes JXL from Safari-only support to majority browser coverage within October. JXL was previously held back mainly because the most popular browser did not support it, which sharply limited its use on the web. With Chrome and Firefox on board, web developers can realistically consider JXL alongside or instead of AVIF and WebP, potentially reshaping the image format landscape for years to come. JPEG XL is an ISO/IEC 18181 open standard that combines a block-transform lossy mode (VarDCT) with a modular mode usable for lossless compression, and it can losslessly recompress existing JPEG files. A practical caveat is that JXL decoding is relatively CPU-intensive, and support in operating systems and desktop software is still uneven across platforms.

hackernews · AshleysBrain · Oct 7, 11:25 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49991227)

**Background**: JPEG XL was developed by the JPEG committee together with Google and Cloudinary, with the "L" standing for "long-term" to signal the intent of creating a future-proof successor to JPEG. Its main rival is AVIF, a next-generation web format based on AV1. Chromium previously removed JXL support in the 2022–2023 timeframe on the grounds that ecosystem interest was insufficient, making this re-add a notable reversal.

## [Paper Challenges Fidelity of LLM's Lean Proof of Navier–Stokes Blow-up](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new arXiv paper (2610.08144) argues that a Lean-formalized proof of finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations, produced with an LLM, does not actually correspond to the original natural-language argument, stating that "the formalised Lean proof does not correspond to the NL proof of blow-up of solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations." The claim effectively challenges a widely publicized AI-for-mathematics result rather than introducing a new theorem. This directly challenges a headline AI-for-mathematics milestone, since OpenAI had presented its Lean-formalized proof as resolving statements C and D of the Clay formulation of the Navier–Stokes problem. It also raises a broader methodological question for the field: how should LLM-generated formalizations be validated, and is faithful natural-language-to-Lean translation itself a new failure mode? According to the community discussion, the paper questions the equivalence between the natural-language proof and the Lean proof, not the correctness of the Lean proof itself, and OpenAI has stated it will not claim the Clay Millennium Prize money. The crux is thus whether the Lean theorem that the checker accepted is actually equivalent to the original problem statement, which commentators note is often as hard as proving the theorem.

hackernews · nill0 · Oct 7, 15:24 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49994145)

**Background**: The Navier–Stokes equations describe the motion of viscous fluids, and one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems asks whether smooth solutions always exist or can instead blow up in finite time. Lean is a proof assistant and functional programming language, based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions, that lets mathematicians write proofs a machine can check line by line. Formal verification guarantees correctness relative to a formally stated theorem, but translating prose mathematics into Lean is non-unique and can itself introduce mismatches between what was proved and what was claimed.
