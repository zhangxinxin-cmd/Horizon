## [SemiAnalysis Publishes Free Teardown of Intel 18A and Panther Lake](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/intel-panther-lake-teardown) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SemiAnalysis has published a free STEEL teardown that examines the internals of Intel's 18A process node and the Panther Lake client chip, taking the analysis from the packaged part down to the bare die. It is the firm's first publicly released client-chip teardown out of its STEEL lab, which it says also covers die annotation, block-level area analysis, TEM cross-sections and full process analysis. Intel 18A is the node that underpins Intel's foundry ambitions and its attempt to reclaim performance-per-watt leadership from TSMC and Samsung, so an independent physical teardown provides rare primary-source evidence about how Intel's RibbonFET and backside power delivery are actually implemented. For semiconductor and systems engineers, this kind of analysis matters because marketing specifications alone cannot reveal real transistor density, integration and manufacturing maturity. The teardown is released free of charge, which is notable given that SemiAnalysis typically reserves its deepest analysis for paying clients and has only been running STEEL — the SemiAnalysis Teardown Engineering & Evaluation Lab — since around 2026, following its debut teardown of Huawei's Kirin 9030 Pro on SMIC's N+3 node. SemiAnalysis says STEEL uses a state-of-the-art tool fleet for die annotation, TEM cross-sections and package-level analysis, though the specific findings for 18A and Panther Lake are not spelled out in the public excerpt.

rss · Semianalysis · Sep 26, 13:36

**Background**: Intel 18A is the second of Intel's so-called angstrom-era nodes, following Intel 20A, and introduces RibbonFET gate-all-around transistors along with PowerVia backside power delivery; Intel markets it for high-performance computing, AI and foundry customers, and also offers an 18A-P performance-optimized variant. Panther Lake is the client processor built on this node and shipped as the Intel Core Ultra Series 3 family for PCs, gaming and edge devices. A teardown like this relies on techniques such as TEM (transmission electron microscopy) cross-sections and die annotation, in which engineers physically strip and image a chip to measure features such as metal pitch and transistor density rather than trusting published specs.
