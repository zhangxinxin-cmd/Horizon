## [FDA Authorizes First Wearable to Continuously Monitor Ketones and Blood Sugar](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-authorizes-first-wearable-device-continuously-monitors-both-ketone-levels-and-blood-sugar) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The FDA has authorized the first wearable device that continuously monitors both ketone levels and blood sugar. The authorization gives people with diabetes a single sensor that provides real-time data on both metabolic metrics. This is a significant milestone in metabolic health because combined glucose and ketone monitoring can help diabetics, especially those with type 1 diabetes, detect dangerous ketoacidosis earlier. It also opens the door for future wearable devices that monitor multiple biomarkers simultaneously. The device is a sensor-based wearable worn on the body rather than a smartwatch, working similarly to existing continuous glucose monitors. Ketone monitoring is most relevant for people in ketosis or at risk of DKA, so the device's day-to-day value may vary depending on diet and diabetes management.

hackernews · sunnynagra · Aug 25, 19:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49439017)

**Background**: Ketones are acids the body makes when it breaks down fat for energy; for people with diabetes, high ketone levels can signal diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a life-threatening emergency. Continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) measure glucose with a small sensor under the skin, and continuous ketone monitors (CKMs) use similar sensor technology to measure ketones in interstitial fluid. The FDA authorization indicates that the device met the agency's regulatory standards as a new type of medical device.

## [OpenAI's Jalapeño chip claims efficiency edge over Nvidia Blackwell](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/openai-jalapeno-better-than-nvidia) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI published first benchmark results for Jalapeño, its custom inference ASIC co-developed with Broadcom. On SemiAnalysis's public InferenceX suite, the 700W chip delivered 1.5x to 1.9x more throughput per kilowatt and 1.7x to 3.6x lower end-to-end latency than Nvidia's GB200 and GB300 rack systems, while sustaining around 550W in real workloads. The results challenge Nvidia's dominance in AI inference and could pressure the CUDA ecosystem, because custom ASICs can achieve better performance per watt and lower total cost of ownership. If hyperscalers like OpenAI prove custom inference silicon at scale, it may reshape competitive dynamics in AI hardware and accelerate the shift away from general-purpose GPUs. Benchmarks covered GPT-OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1 670B, and Kimi K2.5 1T, and the chip used a single-token-prediction architecture with no multi-token prediction or speculative decoding tricks. OpenAI did not compare against Nvidia's newly shipping Vera Rubin, noted the chip is not for training, and plans to deploy Jalapeño in its own facilities by the end of the year, while a second generation is in development and a third in design.

hackernews · Semianalysis · Aug 25, 14:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49434378)

**Background**: Jalapeño is an application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) designed specifically for AI inference, developed with Broadcom. Unlike Nvidia GPUs, which are general-purpose and handle both training and inference, an ASIC is optimized for a narrower set of workloads. Nvidia's Blackwell (GB200/GB300) and upcoming Rubin platforms currently dominate AI hardware; Rubin is expected to deliver roughly 10x agentic throughput per unit energy versus Blackwell. For large-scale AI serving, throughput per megawatt and total cost of ownership are the key metrics that determine whether custom silicon can displace incumbent GPUs.

## [Nitter Shuts Down After Cease-and-Desist Letters](https://github.com/zedeus/nitter/issues/1442) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The maintainer of Nitter announced that the project received cease-and-desist letters, likely from X/Twitter, and all Nitter instances have been taken down for the foreseeable future while legal advice is sought. Development has stopped for now. Nitter was a popular privacy-focused alternative frontend for Twitter, used by many to browse tweets without tracking, ads, or an account. This legal action could set a precedent for how platforms treat third-party privacy tools and affect users who rely on them for access to public information. Nitter only supported browsing tweets, profiles, and search; it did not allow logging in or posting. The maintainer is "seeking legal advice and won't be commenting further on the specifics," according to a message on nitter.net. Other public instances, such as xcancel.com, are also down.

hackernews · Banditoz · Aug 25, 17:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49437283)

**Background**: Nitter is a free and open-source alternative frontend for Twitter that scrapes the platform server-side, allowing users to view profiles, tweets, replies, and media without being tracked or seeing ads, and without requiring an account. Alternative frontends like Nitter exist to preserve user privacy when accessing social media platforms that rely on advertising and data collection. Many public and self-hosted instances of Nitter were available, making it a common tool for privacy-conscious users and for those in regions with restricted internet access.

## [Firefox 157 will enable JPEG XL by default on all platforms](https://groups.google.com/a/mozilla.org/g/dev-platform/c/3YMV4MS34KA?pli=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Mozilla announced on its dev-platform mailing list that Firefox 157 will enable JPEG XL image support by default on all platforms. This marks a significant milestone for the format's browser adoption. This decision is a major boost for JPEG XL, giving websites a strong incentive to start serving content in the format. It may also pressure other browser vendors, such as Google and Apple, to revisit their own JPEG XL support. The implementation relies on the Rust-based library jxl-rs, which Chromium also uses, rather than the original C++ libjxl. Firefox previously offered JPEG XL support only behind a configuration flag, and the new default applies across all supported platforms.

hackernews · yboris · Aug 25, 17:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49437946)

**Background**: JPEG XL (ISO/IEC 18181) is a next-generation image format developed by the Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG), Google, and Cloudinary. It supports both lossy and lossless compression and offers better compression efficiency and responsive web features than legacy JPEG. Browser adoption has been slow and uneven, making Firefox's default-on decision a notable milestone.

## [SpaceX Officially Announces Starbase LA Launch Site in Louisiana](https://www.spacex.com/sites/starbase-la) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SpaceX has officially announced Starbase LA, a new high-cadence Starship launch site to be built near Pecan Island in Vermilion Parish, Louisiana. Construction is expected to begin in 2027, with the first launch targeted for 2029. This is reportedly the largest capital investment in Louisiana's history and gives SpaceX a second major orbital launch complex beyond its Texas facility. The site will support high-cadence launches of Starlink satellites and orbital data centers, potentially reshaping the regional economy and the broader U.S. launch industry. The project is described as a $100 billion spaceport with up to 10 launchpads and will focus on Starship/Super Heavy cargo missions. Its location reportedly offers favorable access to Sun-Synchronous Orbit, which is valued for Earth-observation and reconnaissance satellites.

hackernews · bilsbie · Aug 25, 16:37 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49436822)

**Background**: Starbase is SpaceX's existing industrial complex and rocket launch facility in South Texas, where the company builds, tests, and launches Starship vehicles. The Louisiana site represents an expansion of that operations model to a new state. Sun-synchronous orbits are commonly used by satellites that need consistent lighting conditions, and the launch trajectory from this latitude can be efficient for such missions.
