## [Huawei Unveils 'Tao's Law': Time-Based Scaling to Replace Geometric Shrink](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42966) ⭐️ 9.0/10

At the 2026 International Symposium on Circuits and Systems in Shanghai, Huawei introduced 'Tao's Law' (τ), a new semiconductor scaling principle that replaces geometric scaling with time scaling. The company says it has designed and mass-produced 381 chips under this approach over the past six years, and will release a new Kirin phone chip using logic folding this autumn. If Huawei's claims hold, 'Tao's Law' could open a new path beyond Moore's Law, which is nearing physical limits, by optimizing time rather than shrinking transistors. This may reshape industry expectations for chip scaling, influence competitors' roadmaps, and provide Huawei with an alternative route to advanced density without relying on the most cutting-edge EUV lithography. Tao's Law uses a unified characteristic time constant τ as the optimization target across device, circuit, chip, and system levels. Huawei's 'logic folding' technique stacks layers vertically and is claimed to increase transistor density by 53 percent for AI computing; the company targets 1.4-nm-equivalent density by 2031.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 4, 08:04

**Background**: For decades, the semiconductor industry followed Moore's Law, roughly doubling transistor density through geometric scaling—shrinking transistor feature sizes. As this miniaturization approaches physical limits, progress has slowed and costs have risen. Huawei's 'Tao's Law' proposes shifting the primary optimization target from transistor area to time, systematically reducing the time constant τ across multiple system layers. Logic folding extends this idea in the structural dimension by stacking chip layers vertically rather than shrinking them.

When legitimate companies send emails that look like phishing, they train users to ignore those warning signs.

On June 17, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) published the draft for public comment of the first mandatory national standard for L3/L4 autonomous driving, titled "Safety Requirements for Automated Driving Systems of Intelligent Connected Vehicles," with a proposed implementation date of July 1, 2027. The standard introduces a Safety Case mechanism and sets separate requirements for L3 human-machine handover and L4 autonomous risk handling. This is a major regulatory milestone that shifts China's autonomous driving oversight from loose concepts to enforceable safety constraints. It will affect automakers, suppliers, and policymakers, and may set a benchmark for other countries developing AV safety regulations. The standard requires companies to demonstrate safety through a "claim–argument–evidence" structure in their Safety Case documentation. It is expected to apply to L3 conditional automation, where the human driver must remain ready to retake control, and L4 high automation, where the system must autonomously handle risky situations.

**Background**: SAE defines autonomous driving levels from 0 to 5; L3 allows the driver to disengage under certain conditions but requires takeover on request, while L4 can operate without human intervention in defined operating domains. Safety Case is an established safety-engineering method from industries like aviation that uses explicit evidence to argue that a system is safe. The handover process between the automated system and the human driver is safety-critical and requires careful design and testing.
