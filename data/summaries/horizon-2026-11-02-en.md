## [Terence Tao: Mathematics Has Changed and Must Adapt to AI](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/mathematics-has-changed-lets-act-like-it/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In a November 1, 2026 blog post, Terence Tao argues that AI has fundamentally changed mathematical research and that the discipline should revise its norms and practices accordingly rather than treat AI as a marginal tool. Tao is one of the most influential mathematicians of his generation, so his call could shape debates over credit, peer review, training, and proof standards as AI systems become increasingly capable of producing mathematical arguments. The discussion affects researchers, journals, funders, and anyone who relies on mathematical results. Tao's post is a norms-and-practice proposal rather than a new technical result; the surrounding debate focuses on unresolved questions such as whether someone who obtains an AI-generated proof, understands it thoroughly, and extends it deserves substantial credit, and on complaints that AI-assisted proofs can be long, ugly, and hard to comprehend.

hackernews · smilelamp · Nov 1, 18:28 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50328251)

**Background**: Automated theorem proving is a subfield of automated reasoning and mathematical logic that studies how computer programs can prove mathematical theorems. Proof assistants such as Lean encode proofs in a form that machines can check, which makes it possible to verify AI-generated proofs. In 2024, Google DeepMind's AlphaProof proved statements in Lean at the level of a silver medalist at the International Mathematical Olympiad, illustrating the practical overlap between AI and formal proof. Terence Tao is a professor of mathematics at UCLA who has publicly discussed how AI may expand rather than replace human mathematical creativity.

## [The AI Cost of JavaScript Frameworks](https://nolanlawson.com/2026/10/28/the-ai-cost-of-javascript-frameworks/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Nolan Lawson published "The AI cost of JavaScript frameworks," an analysis of how AI coding agents change the tradeoffs and hidden costs of adopting JavaScript frameworks, which drew a 292-point, 227-comment Hacker News discussion. The piece pushes developers to reassess why frameworks exist when agents can generate framework-shaped code, and the discussion shows that framework choices are tied to hiring, training, and long-term maintainability, not just raw performance. Lawson explicitly says the post is not an end-all benchmark for "I'm a vibe-coder, which framework should I choose?" but rather a demonstration that evidence-based decisions are still possible in the AI coding era; commenters noted that such comparisons will inevitably be read as benchmarks, and others argued that agents do not care about the constraints that protect human teams.

hackernews · luu · Nov 1, 06:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50323088)

**Background**: JavaScript frameworks such as React, Vue, and Svelte provide structure, conventions, and tooling for building web interfaces, and teams often adopt them to keep large, long-running codebases consistent. AI coding agents are LLM-driven tools that can plan, write, and modify code across files with limited human intervention. The debate asks whether agent-generated code reduces the value of framework guardrails or makes them more important than ever.
