## [Claude traces NIST ECDSA curve seeds to lrand48 RNG](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers at Anthropic report that Claude identified the long-mysterious seeds behind the NIST P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384 and P-521 elliptic curves as plain outputs of the C library's lrand48 random-number generator seeded with a Unix timestamp — the P-256 seed, for example, corresponds to August 16, 1997 at 01:52:34 UTC. The discovery was made in under half an hour of model interaction, while the fifth binary seed, B-163, still fits nothing Claude has tried and remains unexplained. The origin of these seeds has been an open question for roughly 25 years and a fertile source of conspiracy theories that the NSA deliberately chose curve parameters with a hidden weakness, so tracing them to an ordinary clock-seeded RNG removes a major cloud over some of the most widely deployed elliptic curves on the internet. It also serves as a striking demonstration of AI-assisted research solving a problem that human cryptographers had left open. Contrary to the ANSI X9.62 description of arbitrary seeds, no hash algorithm was involved in producing these values — they are direct lrand48 output, and rand48 is a 48-bit linear congruential generator with parameters a = 0x5DEECE66D, c = 0xB, m = 2^48. The result confirms how the seeds were generated but cannot prove how they were selected, since candidates that were generated and discarded would be invisible, and one could still search over timestamps to obtain a curve with a desired property.

hackernews · ahlCVA · Oct 16, 18:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122800)

**Background**: NIST published the P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384 and P-521 elliptic curves in FIPS 186-2 in 2000; they are used for ECDSA signatures and ECDH key exchange in TLS, and are also known as prime256v1 and similar names. ANSI X9.62 specified that such curves be generated by taking an arbitrary seed, hashing it with SHA-1, and deriving the curve parameters from the digest output, which is meant to provide "nothing up my sleeve" assurance that no trapdoor was baked in. Because no one could explain where those arbitrary seeds came from, the curves were widely suspected of having been deliberately chosen, a suspicion only intensified by the Snowden-era revelations about NIST standards.

## [Jake Archibald compares JPEG XL and AVIF for web image use](https://jakearchibald.com/2026/jpeg-xl-vs-avif/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Web performance engineer Jake Archibald published a detailed comparison of JPEG XL and AVIF for real-world web use-cases, examining file size, visual quality and encoding performance across photographic, screenshot and illustration content. The post drew heavy discussion from developers and image-compression specialists about encoder maturity and trade-offs. Image formats directly affect page weight, load speed and visual fidelity, so the JPEG XL vs. AVIF decision shapes how web developers ship images for years to come. The debate also matters because browser support is asymmetric: AVIF works in all major browsers today, while JPEG XL remains largely unavailable in the dominant Chromium engine. A recurring point in the discussion is that JPEG XL's reference encoder libjxl is very slow at high effort settings — maximum effort can take roughly 9 to 46 seconds (50–180× the default setting) to save only 2–7% on photos and about 25% on screenshots and illustrations, whereas alternative encoders such as jixel reportedly reach similar results within a couple of seconds. Commenters also argue that published JPEG XL figures understate the format, claiming better encoders shrink lossless files by roughly 30–39%.

hackernews · farlight · Oct 16, 16:12 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50120224)

**Background**: JPEG XL is a royalty-free next-generation image format designed to outperform JPEG, PNG, GIF and WebP, and it can losslessly transcode existing JPEG files. AVIF is an open, royalty-free format that stores images compressed with the AV1 video codec inside a HEIF container, producing much smaller files than JPEG at the same perceived quality. Both aim to replace legacy web image formats, but their encoder implementations and adoption timelines differ sharply, which is what makes direct benchmarks like this one contentious.

## [Mercor: 90% of US Work Hours Lack AI Benchmark Coverage](https://www.mercor.com/research/labor-index/benchmark-map/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Mercor labor-index analysis published as its "Benchmark Map" mapped existing public AI benchmarks onto O*NET occupational tasks and found that roughly 90% of American working hours are spent on tasks with little or no coverage from current benchmarks. It also found that, even after controlling for AI-exposure, O*NET tasks from occupations with larger wage bills are significantly overrepresented among benchmark prompts. The finding raises serious doubts about how well AI capability is actually measured against real work, since the benchmarks used in model leaderboards and marketing may cover only about a tenth of the American labor economy. It suggests AI evaluation is skewed toward higher-paying occupations, which could distort how policymakers, employers, and researchers judge AI's real-world readiness and job displacement risk. The analysis classifies coverage using "unsaturated, public, agentic benchmarks," but critics note that "covered well" is applied loosely — for example, the task "social workers & care -> counsel parents with child rearing problems" is counted as well covered on the basis of only 13 prompts. Mercor's broader ecosystem also includes its independently evaluated APEX benchmarks for coding and scientific tasks, which are separate from this labor-mapping work.

hackernews · speckx · Oct 16, 17:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50121179)

**Background**: O*NET (Occupational Information Network) is a free database maintained by the U.S. Department of Labor that describes hundreds of occupations in terms of the knowledge, skills, abilities, tasks, and work activities they involve, making it a standard reference for quantifying the American workforce. AI benchmarks are standardized tests used to measure and compare how well AI systems perform on specific tasks such as answering questions, coding, or following instructions, and they underpin most public claims about model capability. This analysis sits at the intersection of the two, asking which parts of the actual economy the benchmarks represent.

## [Researchers Exploit 37-Year-Old OpenBSD Kernel Bug](https://calif.io/research/big-fish) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researchers published a write-up titled "Big Fish" on calif.io detailing the exploitation of a 37-year-old kernel bug in OpenBSD, walking through an exploit that abuses predictable structure allocations containing function pointers. The report frames the finding as a demonstration that even a rigorously reviewed kernel can harbor vulnerability classes that code review alone fails to catch. OpenBSD is widely regarded as one of the most security-focused and heavily audited Unix-like codebases, so a flaw surviving 37 years there is a strong signal about the limits of manual code review as a defense. The finding matters beyond OpenBSD because BSD-derived code is shared across NetBSD, FreeBSD and downstream products, and it fuels the debate over whether fuzzing or static analysis could realistically have surfaced this bug class earlier. The exploit specifically leverages predictable structure allocations that contain function pointers, which the write-up notes make ideal targets for exploitation, and the research also covers exploit mitigation in OpenBSD. A notable detail raised in discussion is that the same bug appears to have been accidentally fixed in NetBSD during a rewrite of that code in August 1999.

hackernews · rs_rs_rs_rs_rs · Oct 16, 14:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50118977)

**Background**: OpenBSD is a security-focused, free, Unix-like operating system based on the Berkeley Software Distribution; Theo de Raadt created it in 1995 by forking NetBSD 1.0, and the project emphasizes correctness, proactive security and integrated cryptography. Its code is widely reused, including Apple macOS's PF firewall, Android's Bionic C library, LLVM's regular expression library, and OpenSSH/LibreSSL shipped in Windows 10 — which is why bugs found in OpenBSD can matter far beyond its own user base. A kernel bug is a flaw in the privileged core of the operating system, where successful exploitation can give an attacker deep control over a machine, making such findings especially severe.

## [Show HN: Gemini 4 Argon Plays StarCraft in StarSkirmish Bench v0.2](https://starskirmish.com/bench/v0-2/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

StarSkirmish Bench has been refreshed to v0.2, adding results from the flagship models of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta in a benchmark where LLMs play StarCraft: Brood War by writing code. Each model is given one hour to produce a bot, and models are ranked by how well that bot performs; per community discussion, the resulting order is GPT-6 Astra > GPT-6.1 Sol > Gemini 4 Argon > Claude Opus 5.5 > Claude Fable 5.1 > Claude Sonnet 5.5. The benchmark turns long-horizon planning, code generation, and game strategy into a single measurable agentic task, offering a more end-to-end signal than static question-answer evaluations. One commenter noted that the v0.2 ranking matches the ordering of an independent 9x9 Go benchmark exactly, suggesting that rankings derived from very different games may still reflect something general about model capability. Each model runs inside its lab's first-party CLI harness at the highest reasoning setting available — Claude Code for Anthropic, Codex for OpenAI, Antigravity CLI for Google, and Muse Code for Meta — with web search and web fetch tools disabled and no other internet access, and the one-hour clock starts only after the CLI is installed. Scores are win rates against a roster of competitive human-written bots and demo bots rather than against other LLMs.

**Background**: StarCraft: Brood War is a real-time strategy game in which players must simultaneously manage resource gathering, production, and combat under constant time pressure, which has made it a long-standing testbed for AI planning and control research. StarSkirmish's twist is that LLMs do not play the game directly: they write code for a bot program that then fights human-authored bots inside the game. Benchmarks built around LLM agents, including multi-agent collaboration suites, have grown rapidly as a way to test capabilities that single-turn evaluations miss.
